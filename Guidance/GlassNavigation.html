Glass navigation

Settings → Appearance → Glass left navigation bar (experimental) enables a presentation-only material on the main window's narrow left navigation rail. The rail attaches flush to the screen's left edge and reveals by widening rightward. Only its exposed top-right and bottom-right corners are rounded. It is off by default. The top toolbar, wider adjacent panels, detached windows, image processing and exported images keep their existing behavior.

The blur pipeline and static dither adapt the MIT-licensed OverShifted/LiquidGlass implementation. It is vendored as selected shader source, not an OverEngine dependency. The blur kernel's separate Jam3 MIT attribution is retained too. See source pins and adaptation details.

A small backdrop copy including a bounded 16 logical-pixel sampling neighborhood, two half-resolution seven-tap blur passes and one material draw run before sharp icons. Controls use thin translucent feedback and a slim selection marker rather than nested glass tiles. Their original vectors use neutral ink, slightly larger glass-mode sizing and a subtle opposing-contrast halo. Rounded hover feedback has a soft outer shoulder; selection keeps its own persistent marker. Press feedback immediately compresses the glyph by 4.5 percent inside the unchanged hit target, and release easing is interruptible without delaying commands. Existing reveal timing, panel-expansion and reduced-motion behavior remain in use.

Optical model and upstream adaptations

Stack retains the upstream blur kernel and two-pass arrangement. The attached rail uses one pixel-space rounded-edge distance and normal field for silhouette, refraction and lighting. A rounded convex cross-section with a soft squircle shoulder rolls smoothly into the face; its width follows the rail in physical pixels, with the nearest-edge junction softened only inside the solid. Making the bar taller extends its straight middle without stretching its corner optics or moving the bend point along its full height. The hidden left side continues beyond the screen.

Refraction bends samples inward using the surface normal. Sharp transmitted color is mixed with a small amount of the existing blurred capture. Grazing-angle reflection picks up nearby scene color, while restrained directional lighting gives the glass a curved surface over quiet backgrounds. The edge can darken against a bright field and recede against a dark one; there is no separate perimeter stroke. Static noise is limited to sub-code-value dither. This is a screen-space material approximation, not Apple's private shader or a ray-traced model of the room.

Boundaries and cost

The rail owns one reusable set of three RGBA8 textures and framebuffer objects on the main UI context. Capacity is reserved for the full surface plus its bounded sampling neighborhood and retained through reveal/hide animation. Valid-region clamping excludes unused capacity from both sharp and blurred sampling. The rail is capped at four million source pixels, or 24 MiB including its two half-resolution targets. Ordinary narrow-rail allocations use much less. The material uses three texture samples per covered pixel; the copy, two blur passes and final draw remain the same. No image readback, CPU effect implementation, extra animation or processing-texture mutation is involved.

The framebuffer copy contains display-encoded UI pixels. Blur and material sampling deliberately operate in that display domain, not scene-linear HDR. sRGB conversion is disabled only during this material pass and restored afterward. The canvas outside the rail is not shaded. This is a UI material, not a color-grading operation.

Failure and compatibility

GPU initialization or allocation failure latches a diagnostic shown by Stack's notifications. An emergency solid navigation background keeps controls readable on the failure frame; following frames use standard navigation rather than emulating glass on the CPU. Turn the option off and on to retry. Disabling the option or shutting down releases its GPU resources on the owning context.

This first version intentionally covers only the main-window left navigation rail. It does not change detached viewport rendering or promise Apple Liquid Glass visual equivalence. Normal appearance remains available by switching the option off, including when reduced transparency is preferred.

Verification

The normal Windows build completes for Stack, StackBurst and the Explorer adapter. A temporary hidden-context capture links the production GlassChrome, GLHelpers and GLLoader objects with the pinned ImGui backend. On an NVIDIA RTX 3060, the exact material/blur shaders compile and link, produce a captured frame over a crop of the reported photo/plain background, and report no OpenGL errors. The capture was visually reviewed for local corner curvature and rim shading. This is a standalone renderer capture, not a full-application interaction check. Live navigation, multiple DPI settings, other GPUs and GPU frame-time cost remain unverified. No Apple reference imagery is shipped.

Design references

Apple's Meet Liquid Glass and New look of app icons informed visual assessment: emphasize lensing, preserve transmitted color and use thin foreground control treatments rather than glass stacked on glass. Ten official Apple images covering Icon Composer, clear/dark Dock, Music, Control Center, Safari, Files, Preview and iOS were inspected, together with five useful frames from Meet Liquid Glass. Independent explanations by Kube and Victor Baro informed the bevel/lighting approach; they are approximations, not documentation of Apple's private implementation. NN/g's usability critique reinforces keeping foreground controls sharp and readable. No Apple reference image or code is included. The OverShifted and Jam3 source adaptations are separately attributed as described above.
