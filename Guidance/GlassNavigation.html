Glass navigation

Settings → Appearance → Glass left navigation bar (experimental) enables a presentation-only material on the main window's narrow left navigation rail. The rail attaches flush to the screen's left edge and reveals by widening rightward. Only its exposed top-right and bottom-right corners are rounded. It is off by default. The top toolbar, wider adjacent panels, detached windows, image processing and exported images keep their existing behavior.

The material directly adapts the MIT-licensed OverShifted/LiquidGlass implementation. It is vendored as selected shader source, not an OverEngine dependency. The blur kernel's separate Jam3 MIT attribution is retained too. See source pins and adaptation details.

A small backdrop copy including a bounded 16 logical-pixel sampling neighborhood, two half-resolution seven-tap blur passes and one material draw run before sharp icons. Controls use thin translucent feedback and a slim selection marker rather than nested glass tiles. Their original vectors have slightly larger glass-mode sizing and a subtle opposing-contrast halo. Press feedback is immediate; release easing does not delay commands. Existing reveal timing, panel-expansion and reduced-motion behavior remain in use.

Upstream optical model and adaptations

The upstream superellipse distance approximation, exponential radial refraction curve, procedural noise and angular glow replace the previous independently authored optical approximation. Constants match the upstream application's defaults: shape power 3, refraction exponent 1, noise 0.06, glow weight 0.25, blur radius 2 and one half-resolution blur iteration.

Stack displays the right half of the source lens, treating the left half as continuing beyond the screen. A separate antialiased right-corner mask preserves the requested rail silhouette. Full-scene coordinates are mapped into a bounded local backdrop crop. The source's reversed smoothstep endpoints are expressed as an equivalent descending ramp with defined GLSL endpoint ordering. These adaptations are deliberate differences from the original movable, symmetric demonstration; a narrow attached sidebar cannot look identical to a large floating lens.

Boundaries and cost

The rail owns one reusable set of three RGBA8 textures and framebuffer objects on the main UI context. Capacity is reserved for the full surface plus its bounded sampling neighborhood and retained through reveal/hide animation. Valid-region clamping excludes unused capacity from sampling. The rail is capped at four million source pixels, or 24 MiB including its two half-resolution targets. Ordinary narrow-rail allocations use much less. No image readback, CPU effect implementation or processing-texture mutation is involved.

The framebuffer copy contains display-encoded UI pixels. Blur and material sampling deliberately operate in that display domain, not scene-linear HDR. sRGB conversion is disabled only during this material pass and restored afterward. The canvas outside the rail is not shaded. This is a UI material, not a color-grading operation.

Failure and compatibility

GPU initialization or allocation failure latches a diagnostic shown by Stack's notifications. An emergency solid navigation background keeps controls readable on the failure frame; following frames use standard navigation rather than emulating glass on the CPU. Turn the option off and on to retry. Disabling the option or shutting down releases its GPU resources on the owning context.

This first version intentionally covers only the main-window left navigation rail. It does not change detached viewport rendering or promise Apple Liquid Glass visual equivalence. Normal appearance remains available by switching the option off, including when reduced transparency is preferred.

Verification

C++17 syntax checks pass for the changed renderer, navigation, settings and shell translation units. The exact shaders compile/link on software Mesa; synthetic shader checks establish finite output, unchanged pixels outside the material rectangle and exclusion of stale allocation padding. A bounded test of the production renderer with the pinned Dear ImGui OpenGL backend passes default-off equivalence, panel isolation, transparent rounded-corner preservation, shrink/grow resize, disable/re-enable and repeated shutdown, with no OpenGL errors and restored framebuffer binding. Synthetic renders with grid and broad curved color bands were visually inspected for lensing, bounded blur and sharp foreground glyphs. Production control checks cover hover, immediate press, release-click timing, selection, interruption, disabled controls and the reduced-motion branch; native OS preference detection was not tested. Matched-default comparison against the original upstream shader on Mesa agrees within 8.65×10⁻⁷ RGB over common interior pixels; the original and adapted blur kernel outputs are bit-exact in the comparison fixture. Silhouette and crop boundaries are intentionally different. The legal manifest, retained notices and staging references were checked statically; the Windows packaging generator was not run. These checks do not establish a full Windows application build, physical-driver performance, injected GPU-failure recovery or final visual quality across themes and displays.

Design references

Apple's Meet Liquid Glass and New look of app icons informed visual assessment: emphasize lensing, preserve transmitted color and use thin foreground control treatments rather than glass stacked on glass. Apple's published Icon Composer, clear Dock and glass-clock imagery were inspected as references. No Apple reference image or code is included. The OverShifted and Jam3 source adaptations are separately attributed as described above.
