Glass navigation

Settings → Appearance → Floating glass navigation (experimental) enables independent glass controls in the main window's left navigation column. The column is invisible: it has no background, edge or continuous shadow. Each control has its own rounded optical surface and bounded shadow; space between objects shows the underlying display. Existing layout, reveal and hit targets remain unchanged. It is off by default. The top toolbar, wider adjacent panels, detached windows, image processing and exported images keep their existing behavior.

The blur pipeline and static dither adapt the MIT-licensed OverShifted/LiquidGlass implementation. It is vendored as selected shader source, not an OverEngine dependency. The blur kernel's separate Jam3 MIT attribution is retained too. See source pins and adaptation details.

One backdrop copy with a bounded 16 logical-pixel sampling neighborhood and two half-resolution seven-tap blur passes serves the entire visible column. Individual objects reuse it, so neither another object's shadow nor its foreground becomes a lens input. Sharp original vector icons use adaptive solid light/dark ink, without an outlined halo. Neutral hover/selection feedback and project-pill markings share that ink. A neutral bottom dash identifies keyboard focus, independently of the persistent left selection marker; glass mode suppresses ImGui's colored navigation outline without disabling navigation. Dirty outlines and automatic-project marks retain their meanings. Press feedback immediately lowers the virtual body toward its backdrop and slightly compresses the glyph inside the unchanged hit target, with interruptible release easing. The body retains its curvature and thickness; one normalized elevation changes the optical gap and the projected shadow together. Pointer drag-out releases visual pressure without changing ImGui activation rules, while keyboard activation does not depend on the pointer position. Hidden or disabled glass rendering does not replay stale press animation when it returns. Existing reduced-motion behavior remains in use.

Optical model and upstream adaptations

Stack retains the attributed upstream blur kernel and static dither. The optical body is original Stack code: a shallow closed convex volume formed by sweeping an ellipsoid over a planar rectangle. Circular icon buttons are the zero-core ellipsoid case; project pills are capsules. One orthographic view ray enters the front surface, refracts into glass, intersects the actual exit patch and refracts back into air. The outgoing ray intersects a virtual backdrop plane. Front and exit normals come from the same volume, so thickness changes both magnification and shoulder curvature rather than applying an unrelated UV displacement.

A bounded analytic intersection examines the side/corner quadrics and flat caps, with no unbounded ray marching, scene geometry, acceleration structure or temporal accumulation. The material uses an index of refraction of 1.45 and a half-depth of 32 percent of the smaller object dimension. Reflected rays intersect a finite virtual overhead rectangular emitter. This is a model of an art-directed studio light, not a painted top stripe or uniform outline. Fresnel blends reflected and transmitted energy in linear light. The single-ray budget explicitly approximates untraced internal reflection with the front reflected environment; total internal reflection never becomes a zero-direction texture lookup. Reflected backdrop detail and dispersion are not enabled. Missing capture coverage smoothly returns to the modeled environment rather than sampling uninitialized capacity.

The soft underside shadow is a bounded occlusion approximation from a broad near-overhead studio fill, separate from the grazing strip used for reflection. Its offset, projected scale and penumbra follow the virtual object height and fill geometry. A signed-distance CDF approximation feathers both sides of the projected boundary instead of adding a constant dark perimeter. It is captured neither into the glass nor into image processing. The virtual environment and shadow are not a complete physical simulation, and this is not Apple's private renderer. Original image data and exports are untouched.

Boundaries and cost

The renderer owns one reusable set of three RGBA8 textures and framebuffer objects on the main UI context. Capacity is reserved for the column plus its bounded sampling neighborhood and retained through reveal/hide animation. Valid-region clamping excludes unused capacity from both sharp and blurred sampling. The rail is capped at four million source pixels, or 24 MiB including its two half-resolution targets. Ordinary narrow-rail allocations use much less. The material uses one soft texture sample and at most one sharp transmitted sample per covered pixel; exterior shadow pixels need no texture samples. Each object has a narrow output apron derived from the same projected shadow layout used by the shader, clipped to its approved backdrop column and caller clip. There is one material draw per visible object, but capture and blur are shared. Foreground contrast adds one bounded compute dispatch using the existing blurred capture, plus two small buffers totaling 6 KiB for up to 128 items. Nine samples per item are averaged in linear light, and separate 0.16/0.22 switch thresholds keep slight backdrop changes from rapidly flipping the ink. Persistent identity-based slots retain history while controls reorder or projects scroll; recycled slots reset through identity guards. Fully clipped controls consume no slot. There is no image readback, CPU effect implementation, extra backdrop copy or processing-texture mutation.

The framebuffer copy contains display-encoded UI pixels. The shared blur remains in that display domain. Optical radiance composition converts sampled colors to linear light and converts the result back to display encoding; it does not access scene-linear HDR image buffers. sRGB conversion is disabled only during this material pass and restored afterward. RGB and alpha use separate source-over blend factors so antialiased glass and shadow edges preserve an opaque destination. A narrow, clipped shadow apron surrounds each object as part of the optional UI overlay. It does not change image data, export pixels or hit/layout bounds, and stays in the approved left column, including its shared top toggle when the left panel owns that control. Disabling glass removes the entire treatment. This is a UI material, not a color-grading operation.

Failure and compatibility

GPU initialization or allocation failure latches a diagnostic shown by Stack's notifications. Small opaque cores underneath the individual objects keep controls readable if the material fails, without restoring a continuous sidebar or emulating glass on the CPU. The error remains visible; switching the option off selects normal navigation. Turn the option off and on to retry. Disabling the option or shutting down releases its GPU resources on the owning context. Foreground callbacks require a successful current-frame capture; clipped or off-capture controls retain ordinary palette rendering rather than reading stale pixels.

This version intentionally covers only controls in the main-window left navigation column. It does not change detached viewport rendering or promise Apple Liquid Glass visual equivalence. Normal appearance remains available by switching the option off, including when reduced transparency is preferred.

Verification

The analytic-volume revision passes focused cloud Mesa component checks with the production renderer and pinned ImGui backend on bright, dark and grid backgrounds. Counters verify one capture, two blur passes and one draw per visible object. Gaps outside bounded local shadows and pixels outside the column match material-off output exactly. Input timing, disabled controls, keyboard focus, reduced motion, partial reveals and opaque destination alpha pass without GL errors. The coordinated press update additionally passes pointer leave/re-entry, outside release, repeated clicks, same-frame state reuse, and Escape cancellation while mouse or Space remains held; hit targets remain fixed. Opaque emergency cores are pixel-invisible beneath successful rendering. Earlier adaptive-ink checks cover threshold hysteresis, off/on cleanup, capacity reuse, identity retention and callback ordering. These are component checks, not full-application validation. The actual notification indicator component passes compact and expanded normal/glass rendering, mouse and keyboard activation, reduced motion, adaptive-ink pairing and amber-dot preservation. A shared available-width guard suppresses unreadable compact label fragments while retaining fitting short labels; full-width rendering is unchanged. This does not cover the complete activity panel or AppShell orchestration. Idle output exactly matches the accepted prototype; framebuffer scales 1, 1.5 and 2, shape-matched interaction states and popup capture ordering pass. The press-elevation revision retains pixel-identical idle output against its preceding checkpoint and returns exactly to idle after release on grid, bright and dark test backdrops. Four affected caller translation units pass syntax-only compilation; the project-pill primitive contract also covers its small capsule, stable item identity, selected/dirty/automatic markings and clipping, but not full workspace actions or context menus. A single V5 baseline llvmpipe run measured a warm full-UI median of 1.59 ms after warm-up, while the uncached first material-render wall sample was 424 ms including shader compilation, target allocation and synchronization. The cold fixture disabled Mesa's driver shader cache. With ordinary ImGui already warm, glass enable measured 372 ms uncached versus 18.6 ms with a populated normal cache; deferred first shader execution dominated, not target allocation. These software-driver measurements are not physical-GPU predictions and the warm number excludes initialization. The Windows build, native reduced-motion behavior, hardware-driver appearance and physical-GPU frame time require validation for this revision. A single ink chosen from a local mean cannot guarantee contrast on every stroke over an arbitrarily mixed backdrop; it measures the pre-material capture rather than exact refracted pixels. No Apple reference imagery is shipped.

Current quality tradeoffs

Keep the single-ray material until a replacement improves native-size appearance and motion, not just enlarged still images. Focused cloud experiments did not justify shoulder supersampling, dispersion or an additional internal reflection: five-tap shoulder sampling gave marginal visible improvement with about 27 percent higher warm software-render time in its matched component fixture; separate color-channel rays introduced moving colored speckles; one consistently weighted internal bounce added a localized glint rather than the reference's broad lower shoulder and sparkled under fractional motion. In the inspected glint pixels, second intersections remained valid, but the secondary energy weight varied steeply between samples, so softening only the emitter is not an established fix. None of these experiments is enabled. These findings are specific to the tested geometry and fixtures, not universal limits on those techniques.

Design references

Apple's Meet Liquid Glass and New look of app icons informed visual assessment: emphasize lensing, preserve transmitted color and give each floating control a curved optical surface without a second continuous glass layer behind it. Ten official Apple images covering Icon Composer, clear/dark Dock, Music, Control Center, Safari, Files, Preview and iOS were inspected, together with five useful frames from Meet Liquid Glass. Independent explanations by Kube and Victor Baro informed the bevel/lighting approach; they are approximations, not documentation of Apple's private implementation. NN/g's usability critique reinforces keeping foreground controls sharp and readable. No Apple reference image or code is included. The OverShifted and Jam3 source adaptations are separately attributed as described above.
